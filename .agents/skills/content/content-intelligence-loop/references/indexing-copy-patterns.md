# Indexing Copy Patterns

Use this reference every week. The goal is to learn from the pages Google accepts
versus the pages Google discovers or crawls but does not index.

## Positive copy shape

Indexed examples follow this shape:

1. Exact factual query in the title: "How to see who someone recently followed on
   Instagram", "Does Instagram show who you recently followed", "How to see who
   unfollowed you on Instagram".
2. First sentence answers the query directly: yes, no, Instagram does not, it means,
   here is how.
3. First paragraph names the specific surface immediately: Instagram, Threads, the
   following list, the follower list, notifications, activity status, a named tracker.
4. The post gives concrete lookup evidence: in-app paths (Profile → Following),
   order behavior, notification behavior, what is and is not visible, and a named tool.
5. Emotional context is secondary. It can explain why the reader cares, but it cannot
   lead the piece.

## Negative copy shape

Not-indexed pages often share these patterns:

- Reassurance-first openings: "You are not crazy", "You are not paranoid", "You are not
  overthinking", "Trust your gut", "If you are reading this".
- Abstract relationship framing before facts.
- High second-person intensity with low named-surface density.
- Generic relationship advice without a specific Instagram surface anchor.
- Short posts that do not add concrete lookup evidence beyond the query itself.

## Hard writing rules

For content-intelligence generated posts:

- Do not open with reassurance, validation, or therapy language.
- Do not bury the answer below an emotional setup.
- Preserve the exact factual query shape from title to first paragraph.
- Name Instagram or the specific surface in the first paragraph.
- Put at least two concrete evidence terms in the first paragraph: follow, follower,
  following, unfollow, list, profile, account, username, private, public, order,
  chronological, recent, mutual, notification, tracker, scan.

If a topic cannot satisfy those rules, drop it from the weekly batch. Generating fewer
pages is better than shipping more discovered-not-indexed pages.
